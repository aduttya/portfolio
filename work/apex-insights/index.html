Why I built it

I started with Formula 1 and went down the rabbit hole into GT3. Ferrari, Porsche, BMW and McLaren racing near-identical cars in championships across Europe, Asia, Australia and America, with grids of 50 cars and races up to 24 hours long.

Every race weekend produces a lot of data, and most coverage focuses on who won and the title fight. I like finding patterns in data and making them fun for other people, so I started building the site I wanted to read.

Design

A dark, data-first look built for race weekends. Big type up front, and dense data laid out to read quickly.

Product and architecture

One app that turns official race data into pages, insights and stories. A new race shows up across the whole site as soon as it's in.

  1. 01

    Entity pages

    329 drivers, 74 teams, 25 championships and 11 manufacturers, all linked to each other.

  2. 02

    Race weekend pages

    Practice, qualifying, grids, results and stewards' decisions for every round, with a "follow the weekend" feed that goes live as sessions run.

  3. 03

    Insights and stories

    Pace analysis across the season, and data stories built on top of it.

  4. 04

    Podcast

    Three episodes with their own pages and Spotify and YouTube embeds, stored in the same database as everything else.

Data pipeline and analysis

Growth

The site is built around the entities in the sport and the links between them, rather than a keyword list, and changes are measured before I call them a win.

{%- assign imp = "Jul:797,Aug:1509,Sep:1333,Oct:5346,Nov:5116" | split: "," %}
  1. 01

    Structure over prose

    Index pages use stat strips, breakdown tables and a short FAQ rather than long guides. "GT3 teams in 2026" was one of the first searches Apex showed up for.

  2. 02

    A weekly measurement loop

    Each week is compared with an eight week baseline, with gates that filter out race weekend swings and small numbers. A learnings log grades each claim as a hypothesis, supported or confirmed.

  3. 03

    One change per experiment

    Before shipping 266 driver pages I snapshotted the baseline, then measured the same pages and queries weeks later.

  4. 04

    A clean domain move

    In October the site moved from apexinsights.xyz to apexinsights.racing with permanent redirects and a Change of Address in Search Console. Impressions kept climbing through the move, and the new domain passed 100 clicks from Google within its first month.

Stories, reels and the podcast

F1 has plenty of data-led content, and GT3 has much less. So the data becomes stories on the site, and the stories become reels. Each reel is built as 1080 by 1920 HTML frames on its own small design system, then rendered to video.

Four of 16 reels. Nine data and editorial stories are live, and three podcast episodes.

What didn't work

  • {% include icon.html name="close" %}A 600 word prose guide on the teams page. It was off-brand for a data site, so it was replaced with a structured page.
  • {% include icon.html name="close" %}Hidden headings that repeated the team list for search engines. They created about 38 duplicate headings and were reverted.
  • {% include icon.html name="close" %}Publishing and waiting. A week after launch, 6 of 7 story pages were still unknown to Google. Sitemaps, IndexNow and manual requests are now part of every release.
  • {% include icon.html name="close" %}Accents in race names turned into broken slugs like "n-rburgring". Fixed with redirects to clean URLs.
